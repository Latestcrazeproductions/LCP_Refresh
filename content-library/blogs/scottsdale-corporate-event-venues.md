## Four room types that cover most Scottsdale corporate work

Most Valley incentive, user conference, and awards programs land in one of these buckets. The production brief should name the bucket before vendors quote.

- **North Scottsdale / Scottsdale Road resorts** — receiving docks, golf-cart gear shuttles, house AV liaisons, and rigging charts that vary by ballroom. Chandeliers and soffits affect lighting and LED width.
- **Central Scottsdale resort campuses** — multi-room conferences with general session, breakouts, and gala in one property. Turnover schedules are production schedules.
- **Old Town and boutique hotels** — tight elevator and corridor geometry, limited dock access, and meeting-room ceilings that cap stage height early.
- **Large expo / civic venues (including WestWorld-scale halls)** — long dock hours, different labor rules, and RF environments that look nothing like a ballroom.

Write the room type on page one of the RFP. A breakout spec copied from a general session quote will not survive a Scottsdale resort week.

## Site visit checklist — bring this to the venue walkthrough

These are the questions production actually needs answered before contract signature. Generic ballroom capacity sheets rarely include them.

1. **Load-in path** — truck dock location, golf-cart transfer, elevator dimensions, and whether gear moves while guests are on property.
2. **House AV exclusivity** — what must stay in-house (internet, rigging, power distro) vs what outside vendors can self-perform.
3. **Rigging** — flown points, weight limits, required house riggers, and lead time for plot approval.
4. **Ceiling and trim height** — stage deck max, chandelier locations, sprinkler clearance.
5. **Power** — named circuits for LED, audio, and catering vs “adequate power available.”
6. **Internet** — dedicated program circuit vs shared meeting-room LAN; write the bandwidth number or assume attendee Wi-Fi.
7. **Outdoor segments** — noise curfew, wind, sun angle at show time, and rain call process.
8. **Haze / atmospherics** — fire panel policy per room; do not assume national-brand resort consistency.
9. **Breakout parity** — input list per salon; shared walls and HVAC noise between rooms.
10. **Rehearsal windows** — hard outs from catering, housekeeping, and union or house labor rules.

If the venue cannot answer three items on this list during the walkthrough, schedule a second visit with AV and catering in the room.

## Resort ballrooms — where Scottsdale programs succeed or stall

Scottsdale resort work is less about gear and more about calendar coordination. Production fails when the plot meets the ballroom the same morning as doors.

- **Dock appointments** — peak season (January–April) fills receiving slots. Book load-in before the floral install, not after.
- **Ballroom flip** — general session to gala is two shows. Separate cue files, separate lighting looks, and a written turnover clock with house staff.
- **Presenter paths** — green room to stage distance matters when you have 12 exec walks in one day. Map it with a cart and a stopwatch.
- **IMAG and LED** — sight lines from the last row and camera position drive wall width. Chandeliers steal width you thought you had on the PDF floor plan.
- **RF** — multiple events on one property during convention weeks. Coordinate with house AV before lavs are taped.

Treat the resort AV liaison as part of your production team for the week — not a gatekeeper to bypass day-of.

## Outdoor and courtyard segments — Scottsdale-specific variables

Outdoor keynotes and dinners are common and frequently under-specified. Sun, wind, and resort ambient noise are production inputs.

- **Sun angle** — west-facing courtyards change exposure between rehearsal and doors. Schedule a look check after the sun moves.
- **Wind** — screen, LED, and tent ballast ratings belong on the plot, not discovered at 4 p.m.
- **Audio spill** — pool pumps, adjacent events, and traffic paths are ambient noise sources on the site visit.
- **Power and climate** — encoder and playback racks need conditioned space; desert heat on a rack under a tent is a stream failure.

Outdoor is not a cheaper ballroom. It is a different show with weather as a cast member.

## What to send your production partner after venue selection

Attach venue name, room names, and site visit notes — not just the catering minimum.

1. Property name, ballroom/salon names, and room type from the list above.
2. Load-in map or photos from the walkthrough (dock → ballroom path).
3. House AV exclusivity summary and liaison contact.
4. Rigging approval status and any weight/height caps.
5. Breakout room input lists if the conference spans salons.
6. Outdoor segment schedule with curfew and rain call owner.
7. Peak-season dates (January–April) if dock competition applies.

## From the floor: the ballroom that was two elevator rides away

A North Scottsdale resort general session had a perfect stage plot — on paper. The receiving dock fed a golf-cart path to a service elevator, then a second elevator to the ballroom level, with a 90-degree turn that rejected a single 8-foot road case. The fix was a modular stage deck spec built in Phoenix and a load-in crew that had walked the path twice before trucks left Magnolia St. The show was fine. The lesson was that Scottsdale resort logistics are a drawing, not an assumption — and the drawing starts at the dock, not at the stage lip.

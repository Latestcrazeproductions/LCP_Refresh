# How to Size LED Walls for Corporate Events

Choosing the right LED wall size is one of the first decisions that shapes your general session or keynote. Get it wrong and content feels cramped, distant, or unreadable on camera. This guide walks through the sizing questions planners should resolve before locking a production budget.

## Start with sight lines, not panel count

Before comparing wall dimensions, map how attendees will see the stage. Identify the farthest seat, any obstructed rows, and whether IMAG cameras will carry detail to overflow rooms. The wall should read clearly from the back row without forcing presenters to oversize graphics.

Ask your venue for a seating chart and ceiling height early. Low ceilings may limit flown configurations; wide rooms may need a wider aspect ratio so side sections stay engaged.

## Match pixel pitch to viewing distance

Pixel pitch — the distance between LED pixels — determines how close someone can sit before the image looks soft. As a planning rule:

- **1.9mm–2.6mm:** Best for front rows and broadcast-heavy events where cameras sit close to the wall
- **2.9mm–3.9mm:** Common for ballroom general sessions with moderate throw distances
- **4mm+:** Works when the audience sits farther back and budget is tighter

Share your room depth with your [LED wall production partner](/services/led-walls) so they can recommend pitch and processor paths that match both the room and your content resolution.

## Define aspect ratio from content, not aesthetics

Corporate events rarely need a cinematic 16:9 wall by default. Keynotes with tall speaker logos, vertical social clips, or split presenter-and-slides layouts may need custom dimensions. Build your run-of-show first, then size the wall around the dominant content format.

If you plan live switching between full-screen video, presenter IMAG, and presentation slides, confirm the wall supports those layouts without letterboxing that shrinks effective screen area.

## Account for staging footprint and load-in

LED walls compete for stage depth with scenic elements, confidence monitors, and presenter walk paths. A wall that looks right on paper can steal too much downstage space for awards segments or panel discussions.

Confirm load-in access, power distribution, and rigging points before finalizing dimensions. Ground-supported stacks need wider wings; flown walls need structural approval and often longer install windows.

## When to scale up — or split across screens

A single large wall works when one focal point carries the entire room. Consider multiple displays when:

- Breakout-style seating creates dead zones in wide ballrooms
- Product demos need a dedicated reveal surface separate from IMAG
- Hybrid streams require a confidence feed that differs from the in-room image

Scaling up is not always the answer. Two well-placed walls often outperform one oversized panel that overwhelms the stage picture.

## Questions to bring to your production partner

- What is the minimum readable text size at our farthest seat?
- Do we need processor redundancy for a mission-critical general session?
- How does wall size affect rehearsal time and content testing?
- What content specs should our design team deliver?

A production partner who sizes walls from your show goals — not from a standard package — saves rework in the weeks before load-in.

Breakout AV fails when every room becomes a custom install — different switchers, mismatched mic counts, and a tech team that re-invents the show twelve times before lunch. Scaling across a conference means one kit philosophy, tiered room specs, and RF coordination that survives simultaneous sessions.

This guide covers what planners should lock before the floor plan goes final: how to classify rooms by capacity, what belongs in a standard breakout package, and the vendor questions that separate a smooth turnover from a line at the help desk.

## Classify rooms before you count cables

Not every breakout needs the same rig. A 20-person workshop and a 120-person training block share a label on the agenda but not the same PA, screen size, or mic inventory. Tier your rooms early so procurement, load-in, and staffing math stay honest.

Use three tiers as a starting point — adjust counts to your venue, not your optimism:

- **Tier 1 (up to 40 seats):** Single display or short-throw projection, 2–3 wireless handhelds or one lav plus handheld, small powered PA or ceiling reinforcement, HDMI/USB-C switcher with wireless presenter clicker, confidence monitor optional.
- **Tier 2 (40–100 seats):** Larger display or 5,000+ lumen projection, lav for primary speaker plus 2–4 handhelds for Q&A, compact line array or distributed speakers with one delay fill if the room is deep, dedicated audio tech on call (not necessarily in-room full time).
- **Tier 3 (100+ seats or broadcast):** Dual displays or projection plus IMAG confidence, full mic inventory with redundancy, proper FOH mix position, iso recording or stream feed, dedicated tech in-room for the full session block.

Write tier assignments on the master schedule, not just the CAD. When sales adds a fourth concurrent session on Wednesday, you need to know whether you're cloning Tier 1 or promoting a ballroom to Tier 2 without three days of re-cabling.

## Standardize the kit — same inputs, same handoffs

Scaling is a logistics problem wearing an AV costume. The rooms can differ in size; the signal path should not. Pick one laptop input standard (HDMI at 1080p59.94, or USB-C with confirmed HDCP behavior), one wireless presentation system, and one mic type per tier. Mixed brands across adjacent rooms means mixed spare batteries, mixed mute behavior, and a warehouse of adapters someone will misplace.

Build a breakout kit sheet every room inherits:

- **Video:** One primary switcher or dedicated input on a small matrix; labeled inputs ("Laptop," "Backup Laptop," "Playback"); EDID management so MacBooks don't surprise you at 8:02 AM.
- **Audio:** Defined maximum active wireless channels per tier; charging station with labeled slots; mute groups documented on a one-page show sheet taped inside each rack.
- **Control:** Identical clicker pairing procedure in every room — same button for blank screen, same button for black-out if your switcher supports it.
- **Signage:** Room name, session code, and a single help number on a printed card at the lectern. Not branded fluff — operational clarity.
- **Spares:** One spare handheld per two rooms minimum at the breakout desk; spare HDMI and USB-C cables staged by the load-in dock, not in a truck three levels down.

Document turnover time between sessions. If Tier 2 rooms need twelve minutes to swap laptops and re-seat mics, your 10-minute break is already broken. Either pad the schedule or pre-patch both laptops before doors.

## RF, neighbors, and the general session next door

Multi-room conferences are RF coordination problems. Breakout lavs, audience mics, press rooms, and the keynote next door all compete for the same bands. A dropout in Breakout 4 during the CEO's Q&A in the ballroom is not bad luck — it's inventory nobody consolidated.

Coordinate frequencies before load-in, not at sound check:

- **Master coordination sheet** — one document listing every active channel, room assignment, and backup frequency; owned by audio lead, not distributed across three clipboards.
- **Scan window** — full venue scan after all vendors arrive; adjacent-hotel conferences and venue permanent systems don't care about your agenda.
- **Channel caps per tier** — Tier 1 gets two channels max unless you upgrade; Tier 3 gets documented spares; no "we'll grab another receiver if we need it."
- **Intermod testing** — run all tier rooms simultaneously for ten minutes during tech rehearsal; dropouts show up when everything is live, not when one mic is on in an empty room.
- **IFB and comms separation** — show caller comms, tech comms, and presenter IFB on separate bands from breakout handhelds; comms bleed is how a stage manager's cue ends up in a breakout recording.

If overflow rooms mirror a general session on delay, treat them as Tier 2 minimum even when the seating count looks like Tier 1. Delay feeds, confidence audio, and a tech who knows when to switch from playback to live are not optional add-ons — they're the reason people sit in overflow instead of leaving.

## Questions for your production partner

Bring this list to your RFP and pre-con walkthrough. Breakout scaling breaks at the handoffs vendors assume someone else owns.

- **Kit standardization** — Will every room in the same tier ship identical signal paths, or do subs bring "equivalent" gear that behaves differently under pressure?
- **Turnover staffing** — Who resets rooms between sessions, and what's the SLA when a presenter leaves a laptop logged in and a mic in a bag?
- **RF ownership** — Who holds the master coordination sheet, and who gets called when an unregistered vendor powers up in the sponsor lounge?
- **Power and rigging** — Per-room power budget, projector throw distances confirmed against actual ceiling height, and who owns screen size if the hotel's "built-in" display is a 65-inch monitor at the wrong end of a 90-foot room.
- **Recording and capture** — Which rooms get ISO recording, and is it local or routed to a central record room? Mixed approaches lose sessions.
- **Help desk model** — One floating tech per how many rooms, response time target, and escalation path when a session starts in four minutes and HDMI isn't handshake-ing.
- **Change-order triggers** — What happens when agenda adds a fourth concurrent Tier 2 on the same floor Tuesday night? Price and crew scale how?

Answers belong in the production brief, not in a verbal promise at the pre-con. Breakouts punish vague specs because there are too many rooms for heroics.

## Rehearsal — test turnover, not just sound check

Block a rehearsal segment that simulates the worst Wednesday, not a empty-room mic check:

1. **Simultaneous sessions** — Run at least three tier-matched rooms at once with all wireless active; listen for dropouts and intermod.
2. **Turnover drill** — Full swap between two back-to-back sessions in one room: new laptop, new clicker, mic handoff, slide test on screen; time it.
3. **Walk-in presenter** — Someone who has never seen the room plugs in and goes live with a one-page instruction card only; note every question they ask.
4. **Help desk ping** — Radio or text the breakout desk from two rooms at once; confirm staffing can handle parallel calls.
5. **Fail one path** — Kill a wireless receiver or swap to backup laptop input; confirm the in-room tech or floater knows the manual step without calling the A1 on the keynote.

Document room-tier assignments, kit sheets, and RF inventory where every tech can reach them — shared drive, printed binders at the breakout desk, and a single Slack channel that isn't buried by catering photos.

## From the floor: twelve rooms, one spare clicker

A multi-track conference shipped identical Tier 1 kits to twelve breakout rooms — same switcher, same mic count, same lectern layout. Eleven rooms worked. Room 7 had a projector mounted six inches lower than the CAD, which put the bottom of the image into the first row's hairlines and made the wireless clicker lose line-of-sight unless you stood stage left of a pillar.

The fix was two hours of re-rigging that should have been a pre-con site visit note: one delay speaker, clicker receiver relocated, and a printed "stand here" mark on the carpet. The gear was fine. The room wasn't wrong. The template assumed a room that didn't exist.

Breakout AV scales when tiers are honest, kits are identical within each tier, and RF is coordinated like one show instead of twelve small ones. Spec it that way early, and your floor team spends show day turning sessions, not negotiating adapters.

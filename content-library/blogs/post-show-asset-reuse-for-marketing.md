Marketing’s post-show plan usually assumes the general session becomes six months of cuts, social clips, and sales enablement. Production’s plan assumes the show file closes at load-out so the next date can load in. The gap between those two timelines is where asset reuse dies — not because the keynote failed, but because nobody separated **live playback** from **marketing exports** before the PO was signed. This guide defines what to lock before doors, what marketing can reuse without touching the show file, and how to brief production partners so editors get one coherent package instead of a relitigation of every bumper, safe zone, and last-minute slide swap.

## Post-show reuse is a delivery contract, not a creative do-over

The show file is the operational truth for show day: cue list, switcher layers, playback paths, IMAG routing, and the version that actually ran when the CEO walked on. Marketing reuse depends on a **parallel deliverable set** — isolated program audio, camera ISOs or clean program records, graphics captured at native resolution, and metadata that tells an editor which segments are cleared for external use.

When teams treat “send us the files” as a post-show afterthought, marketing opens the show file by default. That triggers exactly what you want to avoid: debates over which lower-thirds version is canonical, requests to re-export walk-on video at a different aspect ratio, and production staff billing time to rebuild a session they already struck. Reuse works when capture and naming were scoped as marketing deliverables in the original brief — with deadlines, formats, and ownership that do not require reopening live build.

Your production partner should understand three boundaries before load-in:

1. **Show file scope** — What production owns for playback and IMAG through strike. Changes after content lock follow production change control, not marketing’s campaign calendar.
2. **Marketing export scope** — What ships to editors: program mix, presenter ISOs, slide or graphics captures, B-roll where contracted, and any cleared music or third-party rights notes.
3. **No-touch assets** — Elements marketing may cut from delivered files without asking production to re-render the show: full program record, designated ISO angles, static slide PDFs or native deck exports when provided separately from live playback.

Document those boundaries in the SOW. “We’ll figure it out after the show” is how relitigation starts.

## Business outcomes when asset reuse is governed upfront

Executives fund production partly on **content leverage** — the same spend that filled the ballroom should feed campaigns without a reshoot. Finance will not approve duplicate capture budgets next quarter if this year’s footage sits in incompatible folders while marketing hires a crew to recreate the keynote set.

When reuse is scoped before the RFP closes, marketing and leadership typically see:

- **Campaign timeline holds** — Editors work from a defined export pack on a defined schedule instead of waiting for someone to “find the right ProRes from camera three.”
- **Content utilization rate rises** — Planned cuts (hero reel, executive sound bites, vertical social, internal comms) ship because framing, program audio, and segment markers were capture requirements, not hopes.
- **Production change orders drop post-show** — Re-export requests, safe-zone rebuilds, and emergency color passes shrink when marketing never needed the live show file to begin with.
- **Message consistency across channels** — The in-room IMAG frame, the stream, and the post-show cut share the same presenter coverage standards because they were one brief, not three interpretations after load-out.
- **Program ROI is defensible** — You can point to delivered assets, publish dates, and reuse across quarters instead of arguing whether production “should have” recorded differently.

Track reuse at the **program level** when you run annual meetings, roadshows, or serial summits. One stop that skipped ISO routing can poison the whole highlight reel — that is a governance failure, not an editor problem.

## Asset tiers: what marketing gets vs what stays in the truck

Not every moment on the run-of-show deserves the same post-show treatment. Tiering protects budget and keeps production focused on show-day integrity while marketing gets what it actually publishes.

**Tier 1 — Flagship (full marketing export package):** CEO keynote, product reveal, leadership panel designated for external use, awards segments cleared for comms, and any moment named in the marketing brief as record-ready. Deliverables include multi-angle or program record per spec, isolated program audio, segment markers or timecode notes, and delivery within the contracted window (commonly 48–72 hours for raw, longer for graded selects if scoped).

**Tier 2 — Support (program record + slides):** Internal panels, breakouts with slides-only capture, and segments that need archive but not hero editing. Single program feed or slides PDF plus room audio; no ISO camera package unless added.

**Tier 3 — Live only (no marketing pipeline):** VIP dinners, unconstrained breakouts, and sessions explicitly excluded from reuse. Production does not allocate ISO record paths or marketing naming conventions — and marketing does not ask for them in the edit bay.

Before you sign, marketing and events should publish the tier list with segment names. Otherwise every breakout becomes Tier 1 when a VP asks for a clip in week two.

## The marketing export checklist (define before load-in)

Use this as the attachment to your production brief — not a post-show email. Production prices labor and record paths against it; marketing plans campaigns against it.

1. **Program audio** — Dedicated mix bus for record and stream, documented in the tech rider. Specify stereo program, presenter lav isolation if required, and whether music is cleared for cut-down use or room-only.
2. **Video records** — Program cut vs ISO cameras; resolution and codec; which angles are cleared for vertical reframes. Note if IMAG burn-in is acceptable on program record or if marketing needs clean ISOs only.
3. **Graphics and slides** — Native deck or approved PDF delivered **outside** the live show file when possible, so marketing updates supers without touching playback builds. If only live capture exists, document safe zones validated on actual screen size at rehearsal.
4. **Segment log** — Simple run-of-show with timecode or clock time for Tier 1 moments, speaker names, clearance flags (internal vs external), and music cues that affect reuse.
5. **File naming and folder structure** — Consistent across the program: `Program_Audio`, `ISO_Cam_A`, `Slides_Native`, `Segment_Log.pdf`. One shared drive or DAM path; no regional rename drift on tour stops.
6. **Delivery milestones** — Raw pack within X hours of load-out; optional graded selects or string-out if scoped and paid. Missing a milestone triggers debrief action, not a Slack thread to the TD who is already in another city.

If a vendor cannot commit to the checklist in writing, assume marketing will inherit the show file by default — and budget relitigation time accordingly.

## Questions for your production partner (before award)

Qualify on **export governance**, not camera brand. These questions surface whether reuse is a line item or a favor after strike.

- **How do you isolate program audio for record without post-fader chaos from walk-up mics?** You want a matrix path and a named owner for the record mix, not “we’ll send what the board had.”
- **What is your standard marketing export package for a Tier 1 keynote — formats, angles, and timeline?** Compare answers across bids; the cheap bid often omits ISO record and charges change orders when marketing asks in week one.
- **Do you deliver native slide or graphics files separately from the show file?** Separation is what prevents relitigating every lower-third when comms wants a six-second cut.
- **Who owns segment logging during the show — and where does it land before the truck rolls?** Timecode notes from the show caller or TD beat reconstructing the CEO walk-on from memory.
- **How do you handle multi-market tours so folder structure matches stop to stop?** Drift between cities is how editors end up with twelve “final_final” exports and zero usable continuity.
- **What requires reopening the show file after load-out — and what is excluded by scope?** Clear answers protect both sides when marketing requests a re-export at a new aspect ratio six weeks later.

Partners who respond with deliverable dates, folder templates, and record-path diagrams are set up for reuse. Partners who only discuss screen size and speaker count are pricing the room, not the asset plan.

## When to scale capture up or down (without starving reuse)

Reuse is not “record everything.” It is recording **what marketing committed to publish** with enough quality that reframing and audio cleanup do not eat the campaign calendar.

**Scale up when:**

- External comms, IR, or sales enablement depends on clips for more than one quarter.
- The keynote set includes product demo visibility, LED content, or panel dynamics that a single program wide cannot cover for vertical cuts.
- Hybrid or simulive audiences require parity — marketing will compare stream framing to post-show hero cuts.
- You are touring the same content stack; inconsistent capture between stops breaks the unified reel finance already approved.

**Scale down when:**

- Sessions are internal-only with no clearance path — Tier 3 saves ISO labor and storage without affecting brand campaigns.
- Marketing’s plan is slides-plus-voiceover for enablement, not cinematic recap — program record and native deck may suffice.
- Rehearsal and show-day risk need budget more than a fifth ISO angle nobody will cut.
- Rights or talent restrictions block external use; capturing beyond archive need adds cost without utilization.

The decision belongs in the pre-event brief marketing signs, not in the loading dock conversation after rehearsal ran long.

## From the floor: the relitigation nobody scheduled

A leadership summit closed with a strong general session — clean IMAG, on-time awards, stream held. Marketing’s brief promised a hero reel within ten days. The production SOW said “recording included,” which everyone interpreted differently.

Load-out Sunday, marketing opened the shared drive and found a single program file with IMAG burn-in, room ambience on the “program audio” track, and no segment log. The walk-on video lived inside the show file at show resolution, not the vertical crop comms needed. The TD was already on a flight to the next market. Marketing asked production to reopen the project, re-export ISOs that were never recorded, and rebuild lower-thirds at a new safe zone “just for the cut.” Production quoted forty hours. Events said recording was in scope. Finance asked why post-show production looked like a second show.

The fix for the next cycle was boring on purpose: Tier 1 segments named in the brief, isolated program audio on the rider, two ISO angles on the CEO block, native deck delivery by content lock, folder naming in the contract, and a 72-hour raw delivery clause — explicitly **not** access to the live show file for marketing edits. The highlight reel shipped on day nine. Nobody reopened cue 47.

Reuse is not a favor after strike. It is a package you paid for when the brief said so.

Walk-on music and lighting presets fail in the gap between departments: the LD calls "Look 7," the A1 fires "Presenter 3," and the show caller reads a run-of-show that never assigned those labels to the same moment. Numbering cues once — and using that number everywhere — is how you keep awards turnover from becoming improvisation in the wings.

This guide covers what to put on a numbered cue line for walk-ons, how audio and lighting should reference the same index, and the checklist you hand production before the first cue-to-cue.

## Why one number beats three naming systems

Awards segments move fast. A category may need walk-in house, presenter entrance music, a lighting bump on the mark, a dip for VOG, and a reset before the next name. When each department uses its own vocabulary — console cue lists, playlist track names, graphic file IDs — the show caller becomes a translator instead of a conductor.

A single **cue number** (or block number with sub-cues) should appear on:

- The master run-of-show and show caller script
- The lighting cue sheet and console label
- The walk-on music playlist or playback sheet
- Video shade notes when IMAG cuts tie to the same entrance

If the presenter entrance is **Cue 14**, the LD programs **14 IN / 14 OUT**, the A1 rolls **Track 14** or notes **Music Cue 14**, and the wing sheet says **Hold at Cue 14 mark**. No synonyms on show day.

## What belongs on each numbered walk-on cue line

Thin cue lines cause the mistakes you see from the FOH rail: music out before the follow-spot hits, or a lighting blackout while the presenter is still on the stairs. Each numbered walk-on entry should be one row a show caller can read without flipping pages.

**Minimum fields per walk-on cue:**

- **Cue number** — sequential in show order; sub-cues use decimals (14, 14.1, 14.2) only when one presenter entrance needs distinct audio vs. lighting beats.
- **Talent / category** — who is moving, not just "presenter"; include primary and backup if your run allows swaps.
- **Stage path and mark** — wing side, stair, cross, lectern, or center mark; wing staff need this on the same line as the number.
- **Walk-on music** — file name or playlist index, in-point (bar or timecode), out-point or "hold until LD release," and whether VOG ducks or kills the bed.
- **Lighting preset** — exact console name (e.g. `WALK-CEO-AMBER`), in trigger (on music start, on foot on mark, on VOG end), and out trigger (next cue number or "hold through acceptance").
- **IMAG / video** — camera preset or graphic hold if it changes on the same beat; reference the same cue number in the video column.
- **Comms note** — who calls the cue (show caller vs. LD on a look), and any hold language ("Stand by 14, hold for wing clear").

Award shows that only list "Walk-on music: upbeat corporate" without a numbered tie to a lighting preset are asking the A1 and LD to negotiate timing in real time while a presenter waits in the dark.

## Numbering rules that survive rehearsal edits

Cue lists drift. Categories drop, sponsors add a second presenter, and montage blocks move. Numbering rules should keep departments aligned when rows are inserted or deleted.

**Use these conventions in your document template:**

1. **Never reuse a cue number** once it has been rehearsed — insert 14A or renumber the block and reprint; silent re-use causes wrong music on the wrong person.
2. **Reserve a number block for turnover** — e.g. 10–19 category one, 20–29 category two — so wing sheets can say "next block starts at 20" without reading every line.
3. **Pair IN and OUT explicitly** — every `N IN` has a matching `N OUT` or hands off to `N+1 IN`; open-ended "fade when ready" belongs only on acceptance speeches with a time limit, not walk-ons.
4. **Match playlist order to cue order** — if the A1's show file is not in cue-number sequence, add a column that maps cue number to track slot; do not assume the operator will search by talent name under pressure.
5. **Version the sheet** — date and version in the header; show caller announces "We're on v3" when a reprint lands during cue-to-cue.

Planners do not need to own console programming. They need to own the **index** everyone programs against.

## Walk-on music timing tiers

Walk-on beds differ by show tone and venue acoustics. Pick a tier in pre-pro so music length and lighting fade times are decided before rehearsal, not during the first presenter run.

| Tier | Typical use | Music behavior | Lighting sync |
|------|-------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Short sting** | Fast corporate awards, tight run time | 5–12 seconds, hard out on mark or VOG | Bump to preset on downbeat; no slow crossfade |
| **Standard bed** | Most presenter entrances | 15–30 seconds, fade on mark or show-caller "out" | Crossfade 2–3 seconds into walk preset; out tied to cue OUT |
| **Extended walk** | Large stage, long aisle, entertainment tone | 30–60+ seconds, structured out at mark | Follow-spot pickup timed to music swell or VOG end; LD holds look until mic open |

Within each tier, document **who cuts the music** — show caller, A1 on mark, or automated timecode — and what happens if the presenter stops early (hold music vs. kill vs. segue to underscore).

## Pre-cue-to-cue checklist for planners

Hand this completed block to your production lead when walk-on cues are "mostly final." Incomplete rows are the ones that get fixed with hand signals on show day.

**Two weeks out:**

- **Numbered cue sheet v1** — every presenter entrance through the awards block has a cue number and paired IN/OUT.
- **Lighting preset names frozen** — LD has a list that matches the sheet; no "TBD walk look" on rehearsal day.
- **Walk-on playlist or stems delivered** — levels normalized; explicit file names match the cue sheet column.
- **IMAG column filled** — same cue numbers as audio/lighting for entrance and exit shots.
- **Wing copy** — stage managers get cue number + talent + mark only; they should not need the full LD plot.

**First cue-to-cue morning:**

- **Single source document** — one PDF or shared doc version; no parallel "client run-of-show" with different numbers.
- **Cold read with show caller** — caller reads cue numbers aloud through one category; TD and LD confirm their labels match.
- **Backup entrance** — one numbered cue for "presenter absent / alternate wing path" so music and light are not improvised.

## Questions for your audio and lighting vendor

Walk-on execution sits at the boundary between creative and technical. These answers belong in the production brief, not in hallway conversation after load-in.

- **Who maintains the master cue-number list** — production company, client planner, or shared; and who may insert numbers without a full department re-brief.
- **Console and playback labeling** — will LD and A1 literally label cues with your numbers, or map internally; insist on visible parity at rehearsal.
- **VOG and music priority** — duck vs. mute walk-on bed; latency if VOG is live vs. recorded.
- **Follow-spot vs. fixed wash** — does the spot pick up on the same cue number as the walk preset, or a sub-cue; who calls spot if the presenter speed-walks.
- **Reset time between categories** — numbered OUT to house walk-in look; minimum seconds documented so music for the next cue does not start over a half-faded preset.
- **Stream / overflow** — same cue numbers on program mix; confirm walk music does not mask VOG on the broadcast feed.
- **Change cutoff** — last version of the numbered sheet that gets programmed before show day; later edits need explicit re-cue time.

## From the floor: Cue 22 played twice

One rehearsal had two presenters with similar names and adjacent cue numbers. The wing cleared the first mark on **22**. The A1, scrolling by track title, rolled the bed for **23** early because the playlist sort order did not match the renumbered sheet. The LD hit **22 IN** on schedule. The presenter walked into someone else's lighting look and the wrong eight seconds of music.

The fix was boring and effective: playlist order forced to match cue order, track titles prefixed with the number (`22_Smith_Walk`), and a rule that renumbering required A1 and LD sign-off on the same PDF. The show did not get longer. The wing stopped guessing which "presenter walk" was which.

Numbered walk-on music and lighting cues are not bureaucracy. They are the shared clock for the ten seconds when the room decides if the show feels professional.

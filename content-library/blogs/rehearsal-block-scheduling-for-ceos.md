Executive keynotes rarely get a full rehearsal day. The calendar shows a twenty-minute hold between a board dinner and a flight, and production is expected to prove the show anyway. This guide is for scheduling that block: what must happen before the CEO arrives, the order of operations inside the window, and what you cut without pretending the run-of-show was tested.

Twenty minutes is enough to validate marks, mic discipline, slide timing, and one clean exit — if everything else was rehearsed on a stand-in and the wing stays quiet. It is not enough to fix content, scenic, or a switcher preset nobody loaded.

## Treat the CEO block as a sign-off, not a discovery session

Discovery belongs in pre-pro and in stand-in passes. The CEO block confirms that the tested show matches how this presenter moves, speaks, and reads the confidence monitor. If production is still naming cue numbers or chasing a missing video file when the car arrives, the block becomes a status meeting with an audience of one.

Stand-in rehearsal should cover:

- **Full segment at show pace** — walk-on bed, VOG, slides, embedded video, Q&A hold if it is in the run-of-show, hard out into the next segment.
- **Mark and sight-line proof** — teleprompter or confidence monitor placement, downstage edge, riser steps, and where they must not drift for IMAG framing.
- **Mic and IFB path** — lav fit on a body double with similar jacket layers; handheld backup staged where security allows; program mix level the presenter will hear in the room.
- **Slide and playback failover** — one wrong click on a animation, one forced hold on a video roll; confirm who advances slides and what happens if the clicker dies.
- **Wing traffic pattern** — path in, path out, who meets them at the wing, and where assistants wait so they are not in the shot line.

Document stand-in results on the run-of-show: timing per beat, notes on monitor readability, and any cue edits. The CEO block applies those notes to a human who was not in the building during load-in.

## The twenty-minute block: run it in this order

Do not reorder this list to be polite. Each step exists because the next one fails without it.

1. **Two minutes — room and comms** — House at show look (or as close as fire code allows), confidence monitor on, lav checked on the CEO's channel, clicker paired, show caller and wings on calling channel only. No client tour through stage left.
2. **Three minutes — path and mark** — Walk the route they will use show morning, including the last ten feet to the mark. Confirm teleprompter speed or monitor layout; adjust height once, not five times.
3. **Ten minutes — one full run at pace** — Single pass: walk-on through hard out. Applause and pause length included. Show caller calls sub-cues; do not narrate the tech stack while they are on the mark.
4. **Three minutes — one controlled fix** — One issue only: a hot slide, a monitor crop, a mic gain tweak, a teleprompter speed nudge. Re-run only the affected beats, not the entire keynote unless the fix changed timing into the next segment.
5. **Two minutes — exit and handoff** — Confirm where they go after the segment, who has the mic, and whether house holds for Q&A or a black out. CEO leaves; production logs vFinal notes immediately.

If they arrive late, cut Tier 3 first (photo on the deck, "quick hello" to the crew, a second full run for confidence). Never cut the path-and-mark walk or the single paced run — those are the difference between a signed block and a wing-side improvisation on show morning.

## What planners should lock before the hold goes on the calendar

A twenty-minute block fails in email, not on stage. Send this checklist to production and client comms when the hold is booked — not the morning of.

- **Attendance rules** — CEO only, plus one wrangler max in the wing; everyone else watches from FOH or on delay. Extra staff in the wing is how someone whispers "one more slide" mid-run.
- **Content freeze** — Deck version, video file names, and lower-thirds locked before stand-in rehearsal; the CEO block is not for "I brought a new opener on a USB."
- **Run-of-show vFinal location** — One file, one owner, time-stamped; show caller reads from the same doc the video shade loaded.
- **Change authority** — Who can approve a cue edit after the block; default is show caller plus one client approver, not a group text.
- **Contingency if the block cancels** — Stand-in sign-off stands, or show morning gets a protected fifteen-minute re-touch; pick one and put it in the brief.
- **Security and car timing** — Load-in door, elevator, and wing access aligned with the block start; production should not burn four minutes waiting at a locked dock.

Questions for your production partner before the schedule publishes:

- **Stand-in match** — Who provides the body double, and did they rehearse at the CEO's typical pace (not the A2 reading lines)?
- **Minimum viable block** — If only fifteen minutes materialize, which steps above still run in order?
- **Show-morning add** — If the block slips entirely, is there a crew window before doors for mark-and-mic only?

Answers belong on the rehearsal call sheet, not in a recap email after the fact.

## When to expand the block — and when not to

Some programs need more than twenty minutes; most need better prep, not a longer hold. Expand the calendar when:

- **First-time venue or scenic** — New riser, thrust, or teleprompter rig the CEO has never used; add ten minutes for path only, or schedule a separate tech touch the night before.
- **Live demo or fireside with unrehearsed guest** — Demo operator and guest need their own stand-in block; the CEO join is a handoff test, not the first demo boot.
- **Broadcast or heavy IMAG** — Director needs a framing pass with the actual face and wardrobe; stand-in framing lies about key light and spill.

Do not expand the block to solve:

- **Unfinished slides or video** — That is a content problem wearing a rehearsal costume.
- **Marketing's "can we try the sizzle again"** — Schedule Tier 3 after sign-off.
- **Full cue-to-cue for the general session** — That is a different day; the CEO block is one segment sign-off.

Protecting the CEO's time reads as executive-friendly. Protecting production's prep time is what makes the twenty minutes real.

## From the floor: the wing that stayed open

A general session once held a CEO block at 7:15 AM — car confirmed, lav pre-rigged, teleprompter loaded. Stage management had cleared the wing. Then comms added a "quick five" for a photo on the deck with the scenic still wrapped in plastic.

The photo ran twelve minutes. The CEO hit the mark calm and on time. The paced run started at minute thirteen. On slide four, the embedded video stuttered — a file name mismatch the stand-in pass would have caught if the full ten-minute run had happened. Production fixed it on the second partial run. The hard out into the awards segment was never tested at pace.

Show morning, the CEO took an extra question from the floor. The awards handoff started forty seconds late. Nobody died. The show caller just had one less recovery beat than they thought they had bought.

The lesson was not that executives are difficult. It was that a twenty-minute block with twelve minutes burned on Tier 3 is a eight-minute block with better PR.

CEO rehearsal scheduling is stand-in proof first, a fixed order inside the hold, and a call sheet that treats the wing like a studio during the run — not a green room with a view of the deck.

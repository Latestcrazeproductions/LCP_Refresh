Executive town halls sit between a quarterly all-hands and a broadcast keynote: leadership on stage, financial or strategy slides on screen, employees in the room and often on a stream — and every visible glitch reads as organizational sloppiness, not an AV hiccup. This guide defines the production minimums that protect brand credibility when the CEO segment cannot be redone. Use it to tier your format, write a brief vendors can bid against, and know what to test before doors open.

## Tier the town hall before you count mics

Not every town hall needs a touring switcher and a dedicated show caller. Underspecifying saves budget until the hybrid audience hears echo, the back row cannot read the org chart, or a Q&A mic dies during the only live segment leadership insisted stay unscripted. Overspecifying burns money on an internal update that could run on Tier 1. Assign tier in writing before RFP — name the segment that cannot fail.

- **Tier 1 — Internal, in-room only** (division update, leadership Q&A for directors+, single site) — Slides legible at farthest seat, primary presenter on wireless with backup, house PA tuned for speech, confidence monitor or teleprompter for scripted open, 60–90 min rehearsal on flagship segment.
- **Tier 2 — Company-wide + stream** (global town hall, hybrid Q&A, recording for intranet) — Tier 1 plus IMAG on presenter, program audio isolated for stream and archive, multi-camera capture (wide + presenter minimum), key light balanced for room and camera, named show caller, 2–3 hr rehearsal including hybrid return.
- **Tier 3 — Flagship / external visibility** (investor-adjacent messaging, press on site, simultaneous global broadcast) — Tier 2 plus redundant playback path, hot backup on primary wireless and laptop, dedicated technical director with show-day authority, stream parity rehearsal, failover one-pager signed off in advance.

If marketing needs clips for six months of internal comms, you are at least Tier 2 — even when leadership calls it "just a town hall."

## Audio minimums that keep leadership intelligible

Town hall audio fails in predictable ways: lavs that clip on plosives during the scripted open, stream mix carrying room reverb, Q&A the balcony cannot hear, and RF dropouts when breakouts next door power up during the CEO walk-on. These minimums belong in scope as requirements, not "FOH will handle it."

**Every executive town hall at Tier 1+ should include:**

- **Primary presenter path** — lav or headset with a documented backup (second lav, handheld on a stand at stage left, or dual-path wireless).
- **Secondary voices** — one wireless per seated leader on a panel; moderator mic separate if they move between stage and audience.
- **Q&A capture** — minimum two wireless handhelds or a catchbox plus a runner protocol; define who unmutes and who show-calls the handoff.
- **Program vs. house routing** — presenter and playback on a dedicated program bus for stream and record; walk-on music and audience reaction stay off the archive.
- **Coverage at the back row** — speech intelligible at the farthest occupied seat; delay fills under balcony or soffit if the room sketch says you need them.
- **RF coordination sheet** — master scan window, adjacent-room inventory, battery swap protocol, and who alerts show caller before a mic goes dark.

**Add for Tier 2+:**

- **Broadcast mix owner** — named engineer on the stream bus; not a post-fader tap adjusted at lunch.
- **Hybrid return** — mix-minus tested on show hardware for remote executives joining live; echo cancellation verified at rehearsal volume, not in a quiet office.
- **ISO recording** — local recorder plus cloud backup; at minimum stereo program plus presenter ISO.

One spare wireless per four active channels is a floor for town halls with Q&A. Heavy hybrid formats need more spares against the highest-risk segment, not the average segment.

## Video, IMAG, and slides — what the room and the stream both need

Employees on a laptop notice unreadable slides faster than the room does. Executive town halls need sight-line validation against your actual deck — not a generic venue drawing — and IMAG that keeps faces consistent with your internal comms template.

**Slide and screen minimums:**

- **Legibility rule** — body copy readable at the farthest seat (24pt minimum for dense org or financial slides; 36pt for headers); if the room has pillars or a low soffit, document exceptions and mitigation (riser height, extra delay screen, font bump).
- **Aspect ratio lock** — single source of truth for slide resolution; test embedded video and animations in the show switcher during rehearsal.
- **Confidence for leadership** — teleprompter or confidence monitor with final script matched to deck; no "we'll load the real slides tomorrow" for the opening three minutes.

**IMAG and capture (Tier 2+):**

- **Presenter framing** — director or locked shot list; headroom consistent with your stream layout; no unmanned wide camera on auto-framing for the CEO segment.
- **Lighting for dual audience** — key/fill so IMAG faces are not flat overhead shadows; hold house dim until director confirms camera looks correct.
- **Stream parity** — remote viewers see slides and presenter at the same time as the room; rehearse delay and lower-thirds so graphics do not cover chart axes.

Bring this checklist to advance — require vendor sign-off:

1. **Sight-line review** — Completed against your deck and seat map; mitigations written down.
2. **Playback path** — Named machine, operator, and backup for Tier 3; slide advance cues in the run-of-show.
3. **IMAG director or shot plan** — Assigned for Tier 2+; includes presenter, slide full-screen, and audience reaction if capture needs it.
4. **Hybrid slide path** — Remote presenters see the same deck version as FOH; version control owner named before load-in.

## Rehearsal blocks and show-day authority

Town halls compress rehearsal because "everyone knows the content." That is when demo laptops sleep mid-transition, teleprompter font does not match the deck, and hybrid return wakes up only after the first remote executive speaks. Rehearsal is not a sound check — it is the only time comms, HR, and production align on timing and recovery.

**Minimum rehearsal by tier:**

- **Tier 1** — 60–90 minutes on the flagship segment: walk-on, scripted open, one slide-heavy section, Q&A handoff if live.
- **Tier 2** — 2–3 hours: full run on show hardware, hybrid return with remote participant, stream record review by someone who was not in the room.
- **Tier 3** — separate tech rehearsal plus dress; failover drill for wireless and playback; leadership sees final teleprompter text.

**Show-day governance:**

- One named show caller or technical director with authority to hold segments, extend recovery, or skip optional B-roll without a committee.
- Stage manager, director, audio, and playback on one clear comms channel; client-facing updates on a separate channel so leadership is not hearing switcher diagnostics in the earwig.
- Incident log for near-misses — next quarter's town hall improves from the log, not from memory.

## Questions to ask your production partner before you sign

Vendors quote what you specify. Town hall briefs that list "4 wireless, 2 screens" without tier, hybrid path, or rehearsal hours get labor bids — not brand protection.

1. **Which tier are you bidding, and what segment is named as non-negotiable?** — If they cannot repeat it back, the bid assumes Tier 1 labor for a Tier 2 stream.
2. **Who owns program audio for record and stream, and can you diagram the bus?** — House mix separate from archive is not optional when comms clips the CEO open.
3. **What is the failover for primary wireless and playback?** — One page: what fails, who decides, what the room and stream see during recovery.
4. **How many rehearsal hours are protected in the bid if dock runs long?** — Rehearsal is the first line item cut when load-in slips; ask what gets dropped.
5. **How do you validate sight lines against our deck?** — Generic venue photos are not an answer.
6. **Who is the named show caller on show day, and do they have hold authority?** — Marketing should know the name before doors open.

## From the floor: the org chart no one could read

A global town hall ran clean audio and a confident CEO walk-on — until the first strategy slide went up: eight-point equivalent body copy on a wide screen, house lights still at meeting level, no IMAG. The stream audience screenshot the slide within minutes; internal comms spent a week re-exporting static graphics instead of shipping the planned highlight reel.

The following quarter's brief required sight-line sign-off against the actual deck, Tier 2 IMAG for any slide with financial or org data, and a five-minute record-and-review with someone off the production team. Same venue. Same leadership. Different minimums.

Executive town halls are not mysterious — they are tiered specs, isolated program audio, and rehearsal time leadership cannot see on the invoice but everyone notices when it is missing. Define the minimums early, test hybrid and Q&A under pressure, and treat the stream audience as seated in the back row.

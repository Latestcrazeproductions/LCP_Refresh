A product reveal is not one cue. It is a short chain of lighting looks and playback events that must land on the same beat — often while scenic moves, IMAG switches, and the presenter is still talking. If the run-of-show says "Reveal" without naming each step, three departments will execute three different moments.

This guide gives you a planner-owned sequence: the lighting presets to document, the playback layers that feed the wall, and the rehearsal order that catches drift before an audience does.

## Name the beat before anyone patches DMX

Production cannot program a reveal from creative direction alone. You need a written sequence with triggers, not adjectives. "Dramatic" is not a cue; "House 0%, scrim UP, clip `PRD_REVEAL_01` on frame 1 of audio sting" is.

Lock these decisions in the brief before load-in:

- **Reveal mechanism** — scrim drop, lift, lid open, wall wipe, or live demo unbox; scenic and automation depend on it.
- **Trigger type** — show caller on comms, presenter line, timed automation, or manual GO from the TD; each changes how tight sync must be.
- **Audience sight lines** — who must see the physical product vs. the screen hero; drives followspots, backlight, and whether IMAG leads or follows the live moment.
- **Camera path** — which switcher preset is live for IMAG and stream during the reveal; a look that reads in the room can still be a black frame on the back wall.
- **Post-reveal state** — demo lighting, presenter wash, house partial; the reveal is a transition, not an endpoint.

Cross-reference this beat with your [product launch run-of-show milestones](/blog/product-launch-av-run-of-show) so content lock and cue documentation stay in the same document.

## Lighting preset sequence — document these five states

Your LD programs stored looks; your show caller calls them by name. Use consistent preset names in the run-of-show, on the console, and in comms — not "the dark one" and "the bright one."

Program and rehearse in this order:

1. **Walk-in / pre-show** — house level for seating, stage wash for scenic and camera focus, wall on holding content or branded loop; confirm minimum house if notes or dining continue.
2. **Pre-reveal hold** — house down to your approved floor, presenter key if they are still speaking, wall on tease or black, any followspot parked; scenic in pre-reveal position with work lights off.
3. **Reveal transition** — blackout or near-black (define percentage), optional strobe or accent spike, scenic trigger window, sync point for playback; state whether keys on the presenter die before or after the scrim moves.
4. **Reveal hero** — product lit for room and camera, edge light for separation from set, wall on hero content or IMAG on product; hold long enough for applause and a camera push, not just the length of the video file.
5. **Post-reveal reset** — demo look, panel wash, or return toward walk-in; include time for scenic reset if the next segment reuses the same deck.

Each preset needs an in-cue and an out-cue in the run-of-show. "Go to Reveal Hero" is incomplete without saying whether house follows lighting or chases it by two seconds.

## Playback layers that must share the same trigger

Lighting and playback share one moment but rarely one operator. Document who owns each layer and what happens if the primary path fails.

**Media server or show control** — full-resolution hero video, logo stings, and wall transitions; file names must match the run-of-show exactly. **Audio playback** — sting, bed, or underscore with a defined start point tied to lighting GO, not "when it feels right." **Switcher / IMAG** — preset change to product camera or graphic full-screen; confirm latency so the wall does not cut early while the room is still dark. **Backup clip** — same duration and framing as the primary reveal video, loaded and tested on a secondary channel before doors.

Sync tolerance belongs in writing: frame-accurate for timed automation, half-beat acceptable for a live button press, or presenter-driven with the show caller chasing scenic. If marketing wants a three-second logo resolve on the wall while the lid is still opening, say so — that is two triggers, not one.

For codec, resolution, and lock dates on those files, align with your team on [content lock tiers for event playback](/blog/content-lock-deadlines-for-event-playback) so the reveal clip is on the server before cue-to-cue, not during it.

## Questions for your lighting and playback vendors

Bring these to the first production meeting so the sequence is bid and rehearsed as one beat:

- **Preset naming sheet** — list of looks, console labels, and run-of-show names; who can add a look after rehearsal lock?
- **Master GO vs. staggered cues** — single executive cue or LD then TD then scenic; who holds if one leg fails?
- **Blackout definition** — zero house, 5% aisle emergency, or presenter silhouette; camera and safety implications.
- **Wall state during scenic motion** — black, freeze last frame, or pre-roll hidden; avoids flash frames when the server rolls.
- **Timecode vs. manual** — which layers follow timecode and which follow comms; what breaks if the presenter adds thirty seconds of applause.
- **Failover drill** — kill primary server output once in rehearsal; confirm backup path and who calls it without debating on headset.
- **Focus and camera gate** — when product lighting is focused relative to scenic completion; confirm IMAG shading sees skin and product metal, not just the key light meter.

Answers should live in the run-of-show appendix, not in a side thread the show caller sees once.

## Rehearsal order — run the chain, not the gear

Gear checks prove circuits work. Reveal rehearsal proves the sequence works at show pace with the actual presenter and the actual files.

Run these passes in order before you declare the reveal locked:

1. **Preset-only pass** — LD walks through all five states on comms while playback holds a placeholder; scenic completes its move on each reveal transition.
2. **Playback-only pass** — video and audio hit the sync point while lighting holds pre-reveal; confirms file length, audio embed, and wall aspect.
3. **Cold reveal ×3** — full chain from pre-reveal hold through post-reveal reset, no warm-up between takes; note drift in timing or naming.
4. **IMAG and stream check** — watch the switcher output on a monitor at IMAG scale; adjust product key and backlight before you chase it with camera iris alone.
5. **Reset timing** — measure scenic and lighting return to walk-in or demo state; if reset exceeds the run-of-show gap, add a hold or crew.

Document the winning take's trigger wording on the run-of-show. "After the line" is how you get a reveal on the wrong sentence.

## From the floor: the hero look that was one preset late

Dress rehearsal opened on a clean pre-reveal hold — house out, tease on the wall, presenter lit. The show caller called the reveal on the practiced line. Scenic moved on time. The clip rolled. The product was visible for exactly the length of the video because the LD was still in "Pre-reveal hold" when the scrim cleared — hero wash came up eight seconds late, after the audience had already seen the hardware in work light spill.

Nobody missed a cue. They hit different cues that were never listed as separate steps.

The fix was splitting "Reveal transition" and "Reveal hero" in the run-of-show and requiring both names in the same comms breath: transition GO, then hero GO when scenic reports set. The clip did not change. The eighth second did.

Product reveal lighting and playback reward planners who write the sequence, not the adjective. Preset names, file names, triggers, and rehearsal passes are the deliverable.

Multi-day conferences live or die in the gaps — the ninety minutes between a general session and a seated lunch, the overnight flip from theater seating to classroom rounds, or the parallel reset when three ballrooms change format at once. Conference room changeover AV fails when the agenda assumes instant magic and the floor plan never labeled who moves furniture, who patches video, and who owns the clock.

This playbook covers what seasoned planners lock before load-in: how to classify changeover types, how much time each class actually needs, what belongs on the master schedule, and the vendor questions that prevent a "quick reset" from becoming the reason your 2:00 PM keynote starts at 2:19.

## Name the changeover before you sell the break

Not every turnover is the same job. Mixing a "soft reset" (new presenter, same set) with a "hard flip" (new room geometry, new PA aim, new lighting look) on the same fifteen-minute break guarantees one room wins and two rooms call the help desk.

Use four changeover classes on your master schedule — every session block gets a letter, not a vague "turn":

- **Type A — Soft:** Same stage layout, same seating, mic swap and slide test only. Target 8–15 minutes with a dedicated A2 or stage tech; no furniture crew.
- **Type B — Format shift, same room:** Theater to classroom, panel to keynote, or removal of risers; furniture moves with AV repatch (lectern position, confidence monitor aim, delay speaker mutes). Target 45–90 minutes depending on seat count.
- **Type C — Full reprogram:** New lighting looks, new IMAG routing, LED content change, or switching from playback-heavy to live multi-mic; needs LD and TD in the room, not just a floater. Target 90–120 minutes minimum — longer if rigging or flown elements move.
- **Type D — Strike and rebuild:** Different ballrooms, expo build overlapping general session strike, or overnight deck and screen reconfig. Treat as a load-in window with its own call time, power schedule, and union or venue rules — not a "break."

If marketing publishes a ten-minute break between a Type C and a Type A in the same ballroom, production is not being slow. The schedule is lying. Reconcile session types with break lengths before the agenda goes to attendees.

## Time budgets planners should defend

Venues quote "flip time" in brochures. Your vendor quotes labor in eight-hour days. The gap between those numbers is where conference changeover AV breaks. Build turnaround tiers into the production calendar and hold them when sales adds a seventh general session.

Realistic turnaround targets (adjust for union jurisdiction, rigging, and seat count — these are planning anchors, not guarantees):

1. **Mic and lectern only (Type A):** 10 minutes with pre-mapped channels, spare handheld at the mark, and a show caller who does not allow "just one more photo" on the deck.
2. **Seating reset under 400 seats (Type B):** 60 minutes with a furniture crew that starts on cue — not when catering clears — plus 15 minutes for AV walk-through (screen sight lines, wireless clicker line-of-sight, lav fit for the next speaker).
3. **Lighting and video reprogram (Type C):** 2 hours with LD and video shade on comms, presets loaded before doors, and a hard content lock so the TD is not rebuilding lower-thirds during the flip.
4. **Overnight ballroom rebuild (Type D):** 6–10 hours with a written sequence — strike path, dock access, power disconnect order — signed by venue and production. Anything shorter is a negotiation, not a plan.

Pad adjacent rooms when one ballroom runs Type D. Sound, RF, and comms bleed do not respect session boundaries. A keynote rehearsal in Ballroom A while Ballroom B strikes a flown LED wall is how your wireless scan goes invalid at 7 AM.

## Master-schedule locks for multi-day flips

Changeover chaos is usually a document problem. The CAD shows one layout; the run-of-show still says "as loaded"; catering thinks lunch ends when dessert clears. Lock these on the master schedule and production brief so every department reads the same version.

Items to assign an owner and timestamp before the conference app ships:

- **Room state per block** — seating style, stage dimensions, screen or LED configuration, and changeover class (A–D) for the transition into and out of the block.
- **Furniture and rigging lead** — who moves chairs, when risers can fly, and whether the venue house crew or your vendor touches stacks; gaps here add thirty minutes without touching a cable.
- **Power and rigging windows** — when motors can move, when dimmers can snap to a new look, and when the venue forbids noise toward guest floors.
- **AV repatch list** — lectern location, HDMI/USB-C input labels, wireless channel map for the next session, and whether IMAG switches to a new camera preset or holds a graphic.
- **Spares and floaters** — how many techs roam between rooms during flip windows, radio channel for "turnover lead," and escalation when a Type B runs long.
- **Client-facing hold** — doors time vs. "presenter ready" time; attendees should not watch a deck rebuild because the published start was optimistic.

Publish a one-page turnover sheet per ballroom: previous session out, next session in, changeover class, crew call, and hard doors. Tape it at the tech table and the show-caller position. If it lives in one producer's inbox, the substitute on day three will guess.

## Questions for your AV and production partner

Conference changeover AV is where scope splits — house AV, rental vendor, furniture, and show management each assume someone else resets the room. Bring these to contract and pre-con walkthrough:

- **Changeover class ownership** — Who signs off that a room is "show ready," and what is tested (mic check, slide, lighting look, record path) before doors?
- **Parallel flips** — Maximum simultaneous Type B or C rooms your crew can run with shared RF and power; what triggers extra labor or a schedule change.
- **Furniture vs. AV sequence** — Whether chairs move before or after screen height changes; projectors that cannot stay hot during stack moves need a defined power-down window.
- **Preset and show file naming** — Session codes tied to lighting and video presets so a TD is not searching "Day 2 keynote FINAL v3" during a forty-minute flip.
- **Breakout mirroring** — When overflow rooms flip from local presenter to GS delay, who switches the feed and confirms audio latency before doors.
- **Strike overlap** — Expo or sponsor build into a live general session next door; noise, RF, and dock access rules in writing.
- **Change-order triggers** — Agenda adds a Type C between two Type A sessions with a twenty-minute break; price, crew, and which session moves.

Answers belong in the production calendar and turnover sheets — not in a pre-con slide deck everyone forgets.

## Rehearse one hard flip, not twelve easy mic checks

Block rehearsal time on the worst realistic turnover — Type B or C in the actual ballroom, with furniture crew, the next session's presenter stand-in, and comms live. Run the sequence at pace: out cue, strike path clear, reset, repatch, lighting look, mic map, slide on screen, doors. Time it. If it exceeds the published break, fix the schedule or upgrade the crew before day one.

Soft resets (Type A) can be trained in five minutes at the callboard. Hard flips are where multi-day programs lose hours they never get back.

## From the floor: the lunch that ate the keynote

A three-day conference booked a general session until 12:00, a seated lunch until 1:00, and a keynote in the same ballroom at 1:00 — all theater seating, same stage, "just a quick reset." Catering needed twenty minutes after the last tray left to pull linens. Furniture couldn't start until the floor was clear. AV lost fifteen minutes waiting to reposition the confidence monitor because the lectern moved six feet and nobody updated the RF antenna aim.

The keynote started with house lights still on a lunch preset and one wireless channel still mapped to the panel moderator who had already flown home.

The fix was not faster techs. It was reclassifying the transition as Type B with a 1:15 doors time on the attendee app, a turnover lead with a radio, and a rule that AV repatch starts when furniture hits the fifty-yard line — not when the room looks empty from the door.

Multi-day conference changeover AV is schedule hygiene: honest changeover classes, defended time budgets, turnover sheets every ballroom shares, and one rehearsed hard flip so day two doesn't invent new physics.

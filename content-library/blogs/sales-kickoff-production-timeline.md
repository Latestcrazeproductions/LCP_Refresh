Sales kickoffs compress a year of messaging into three days of general sessions, breakouts, and awards moments — and the production decisions you defer past eight weeks out are the ones that become change orders, overnight builds, or a CEO walk-on without working IMAG. Eight weeks is the practical cutoff where gear inventory, union labor, and freight lanes still have options.

This guide maps what to lock at the T-8 mark: show format and room count, AV and staging scope, content and rehearsal milestones, and the vendor commitments that keep a kickoff from becoming a rescue mission in the loading dock.

## Lock show format before you lock gear

Kickoffs fail in the brief, not the ballroom. Marketing wants a rally; sales ops wants breakout certifications; finance wants a tight hotel bill. Production can only quote one reality. At eight weeks, the agenda shape should be frozen enough that your vendor can hold inventory and crew against actual room count, not a spreadsheet that changes every Monday.

Decisions that must be locked or explicitly tiered by T-8:

- **General session count and duration** — How many plenary blocks, how long each, and whether they repeat (same show twice) or evolve (day-one rally vs day-two training).
- **Breakout tier map** — How many concurrent sessions per block, capacity per room, and whether overflow rooms run on delay from the main stage.
- **Awards and recognition moments** — Walk-on count, band vs playback, trophy handoffs, and whether recognition runs in general session or a separate evening event.
- **Evening events** — Dinner production level (speech only vs full band), separate AV vendor or shared crew, and load-in overlap with daytime general session strike.
- **Hybrid or stream requirements** — Which sessions are captured, whether remote reps get a dedicated feed, and who owns the encoder vs the in-room show.

If any of these are still "TBD" at eight weeks, your vendor is quoting contingency — and you are paying for it whether or not you use it.

## AV, staging, and scenic — hold inventory now

Sales kickoffs lean heavy on IMAG, bold scenic, and LED-as-brand. That gear books in blocks; the week your company picks is the same week three other enterprise kickoffs want 40-foot walls and twelve breakout kits. Eight weeks out is when you confirm dimensions, not when you discover the ceiling is 14 feet and the wall spec was 16.

Lock these production elements at T-8:

- **Stage footprint and deck height** — Panel count, CEO position, band or DJ placement, and whether you need a thrust for awards walk-ons.
- **LED or projection scope** — Wall size and pixel pitch (or projection surface and throw distance), content aspect ratio, processor redundancy for live demos, and delay screen count if the room is deep.
- **Audio design** — FOH vs distributed reinforcement, subwoofer placement for playback-heavy shows, RF channel count across general session plus breakouts, and comms/IFB for executives and show caller.
- **Lighting look** — Keynote wash vs full rock-and-roll, audience lighting for camera, and whether scenic includes programmable fixtures or static looks only.
- **Breakout kit standard** — Tier definitions (see our [breakout room AV scaling guide](/blog/breakout-room-av-that-scales)) so every concurrent room is quoted from the same kit sheet, not rebuilt per agenda line.

Bring a one-page room diagram to your production partner at this milestone: stage, screens, FOH, camera positions, and breakout floor stack. Vendors can work from a napkin sketch if the decisions on it are real.

## Content pipeline and rehearsal — the timeline nobody puts on the calendar

Kickoff content is not one deck. It is keynote slides, sizzle reels, product demos with live switching, awards graphics, walk-up music, lower-thirds for forty VPs, and breakout templates that match the main stage brand. Eight weeks out is when you assign owners and hard dates — not when you ask the motion team how fast they can turn around sixteen lower-thirds.

Content and rehearsal milestones to lock at T-8:

1. **Master content calendar** — Final delivery dates for every asset type: keynote decks, video files, demo builds, awards names, and hold music. Include format specs (resolution, frame rate, safe zones for LED).
2. **Technical rehearsal date** — Full AV run with all playback, switching, and IMAG paths; not a content-only run-through in a conference room.
3. **Dress rehearsal / cue-to-cue** — Executives on stage, mics live, lighting looks called, awards sequence timed. Block this before travel day chaos.
4. **Show caller and stage management** — Named roles, comms channel plan, and who has authority to stop a segment when demo software hangs.
5. **Backup paths documented** — Secondary laptop, offline video of the demo, printed run sheet if the prompter dies. Hope is not a failover strategy.

Planners who treat rehearsal as optional "if we have time" discover at 7 AM on day one that the CMO's video is ProRes and the playback machine wanted H.264. Format mismatches are cheap to fix at eight weeks. They are expensive at midnight.

## Questions for your production partner at T-8

Use this list in your kickoff production review. If the answers are vague, you are not locked — you are hoping.

- **Inventory hold** — What gear is reserved in writing, and what triggers a release if scope slips?
- **Crew lead time** — Are A1, V1, lighting director, and show caller confirmed, or on a "first available" list?
- **Breakout scaling** — Can your vendor crew the concurrent session count on your worst agenda block, including turnover staff between sessions?
- **Change-order thresholds** — What scope changes (extra breakout, larger wall, second general session) require re-quote vs in-contract flexibility?
- **Venue coordination** — Who owns power distribution, rigging points, and union rules — your vendor or the hotel AV liaison?
- **Stream and record** — Separate crew for capture, or pulled from floor techs already running the live show?

Answers belong in the production agreement and the run-of-show skeleton — not in a Slack thread the week before load-in.

## From the floor: the kickoff that added a general session on week six

A technology sales kickoff locked a single general session and eight Tier 1 breakouts at twelve weeks out — clean scope, clean quote. At six weeks, regional leadership added a second plenary on day two for "a quick product deep-dive." Same ballroom, same crew window, but now a full strike-and-reset between sessions, a second LED configuration, and RF inventory that had already shipped to another client.

The show went fine. The production team just stopped pretending the timeline was ever real. The planner's lesson, delivered without much sympathy: the second general session was not a content add. It was a second show.

Eight weeks is the last point where kickoff production still behaves like planning instead of triage. Lock format, hold gear, date your content, and book rehearsal like it is a general session slot — because it is.
